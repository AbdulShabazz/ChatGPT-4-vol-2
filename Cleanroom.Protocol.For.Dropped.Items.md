**Q: What is the proper cleanroom protocol for when you accidentally drop an essential item on the floor?**

**A:** **Result:** A dropped item is treated as contaminated and must be removed from use until it is either properly reprocessed or replaced.

**Standard cleanroom protocol (ISO 14644 / GMP-aligned):**

1. **Do not pick up and reuse.** Floor contact is considered gross contamination.
2. **Isolate immediately.** Prevent the item from contacting clean surfaces or product.
3. **Remove from clean area.** Place in a designated contaminated/dirty container or bag.
4. **Disposition decision (by SOP):**

   * **Single-use items:** Discard.
   * **Reusable tools/equipment:** Send for approved cleaning, disinfection, and if required, sterilization.
   * **Critical items without reprocessing path:** Replace.
5. **Glove protocol:** If gloves contacted the floor or item, remove gloves and don a new sterile/clean pair per gowning SOP.
6. **Surface response:** If the dropped item contacted product or critical surfaces, follow deviation and cleaning procedures for affected areas.
7. **Documentation:** Record the incident if required (especially in GMP/regulated environments).
8. **Re-entry:** Only reintroduce the item after it has passed the defined cleaning/sterility verification for the room class.

**Notes by environment:**

* **Semiconductor cleanrooms:** Reclean via certified wet bench/ultrasonic process; particle counts drive acceptance.
* **Pharma/biotech (GMP):** Treat as deviation; QA disposition required; sterility assurance level governs re-use.
* **Hospital OR/ISO Class 5–7:** Assume non-sterile; reprocess via Central Sterile or discard.

If you want this tailored to a specific standard (ISO class, GMP vs R&D, sterile vs non-sterile, tool type), request an expanded version.

**Q: How are hand tools, vacuums and essential power tools to be handled in a cleanroom?**

**A:** **Result:** Hand tools, vacuums, and power tools must be cleanroom-qualified, controlled, and handled under defined Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) to prevent particle, chemical, and electrostatic contamination.

---

## 1. Hand tools (wrenches, tweezers, screwdrivers, torque tools)

**Requirements**

* **Cleanroom-rated materials:** Stainless steel, anodized aluminum, PEEK, PTFE. No flaking coatings.
* **Low-shedding:** No loose fibers, rubberized grips, or painted surfaces unless certified.
* **Dedicated sets:** Segregated by room class; no cross-use with non-clean areas.

**Handling**

* Stored in **cleanroom containers** (sealed trays, bags, cabinets).
* **Wipedown** before entry (e.g., IPA, DI water per SOP).
* **Dropped tool = contaminated:** remove, re-clean, or replace.
* **Gloves:** Change gloves after tool handling if SOP requires (common in ISO ≤6 / GMP).

**Special**

* **Torque tools:** Periodic calibration; cleaning must not affect calibration marks.
* **ESD-sensitive areas:** Use ESD-safe tools only.

---

## 2. Vacuums (for particulate control)

**Requirements**

* **Cleanroom-certified vacuum only.**

  * **HEPA** (ISO ≤7) or **ULPA** (ISO ≤5) exhaust filtration.
  * Fully sealed housings; no exhaust leakage.
* **Conductive hoses** if ESD control is required.

**Handling**

* Used **only** for approved tasks (particles, not liquids unless rated).
* **Filter change and maintenance** performed outside the cleanroom or in designated service zones.
* **Pre-use inspection:** Verify filter integrity, seals, and certification label.

**Prohibited**

* Household/shop vacuums.
* Blowing debris with compressed air (unless ultra-filtered, ionized, and explicitly allowed).

---

## 3. Power tools (drills, drivers, soldering, heat tools)

**Requirements**

* **Cleanroom-rated or approved by deviation.**
* **Brushless motors preferred** (reduced particle generation).
* **Low outgassing lubricants** only.
* **ESD-safe** where electronics are present.

**Handling**

* **Restricted use:** Only when necessary; often time-boxed and localized.
* **Tool covers or shrouds** may be required.
* **Vacuum assist** during operation to capture particles.
* **Post-use cleaning:** Wipe tool and surrounding surfaces.

**Charging / Power**

* Batteries charged **outside** the cleanroom unless charging stations are qualified.
* Cords managed to avoid floor contact and particle generation.

---

## 4. Entry, storage, and lifecycle control (all tools)

* **Incoming inspection:** Clean, bag, label before first entry.
* **Labeling:** Room class, owner, last clean date.
* **Storage:** Off the floor; closed cabinets or racks.
* **Lifecycle:** Defined cleaning frequency; retirement criteria for worn tools.

---

## 5. Environment-specific emphasis

* **Semiconductor (ISO 3–6):** Extreme particle control; ULPA vacuums; near-zero tolerance for non-qualified tools.
* **Pharma / Biotech GMP:** Documentation, deviation control, and QA approval dominate; sterility may be required.
* **R&D / ISO 7–8:** More flexibility, but still no consumer tools without approval.
