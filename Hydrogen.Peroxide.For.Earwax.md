**Q: Does hydrogen peroxide readily dissolve ear wax?**

**A:** **Yes—hydrogen peroxide can help soften and break down earwax, but it does not truly “dissolve” it.**

**Mechanism**

* Typical use is **3% hydrogen peroxide**.
* In the ear canal it releases oxygen bubbles that **mechanically loosen and fragment cerumen** (earwax).
* The bubbling can lift debris and allow wax to be flushed or drain out.

**Effectiveness**

* Works best on **soft or moderately impacted cerumen**.
* Less effective on **hard, long-standing impactions**.
* Comparable in efficacy to other cerumenolytics (saline, mineral oil, glycerin) for many patients.

**Limitations / Risks**

* Does **not chemically dissolve** wax; it **softens and dislodges** it.
* Overuse can **irritate the ear canal**, cause dryness, or transient dizziness.
* **Avoid** if there is a **perforated eardrum**, active infection, ear surgery history, or significant pain.

**Practical note**

* If symptoms persist after a few applications, **professional irrigation or manual removal** is preferred.
